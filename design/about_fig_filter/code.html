Chalk Lane Ledger · Est. 2022

Flour on our sleeves, smoke in the courtyard.

Two years ago we took over an old picture-framing shop on Chalk Lane. The floorboards were bowed, the windows rattled, and out back stood a gnarled, stubborn fig tree shading a quiet brick patio.

We decided against doing ten mediocre things. Instead, we committed to doing just two with stubborn, unhurried precision: roasting exceptional coffee beans and baking real pastry completely from scratch.

No shortcuts. No frozen dough. No commercial syrup pumps.
Kitchen · 05:40 AM

Mara

Head Baker & Founder

Mara arrives on Chalk Lane while the streetlamps are still buzzing and the lane is dead quiet. By 5:40am, the hearth oven is ticking up to temperature and the room smells of brown butter and yeast.

Her three-day sourdough croissants, crushed-cardamom buns, and roasted fig tarts hit the front counter warm just as we unlock the bolt at 7:30am. When they run out for the day, they are simply out.

timer "Dough doesn’t care about your schedule; it only cares about patience."
Roastery · Small Batch

Dev

Roaster & Co-Founder

Dev roasts behind the counter on our vintage 1968 cast-iron drum. He buys directly from independent farm cooperatives in Huila, Yirgacheffe, and Nyeri, paying well above fair-trade floor rates.

He roasts for natural sweetness, stone fruit acidity, and floral notes rather than dark charred bitterness. If a lot tastes delicate, he lets it stay delicate.

The Roasting Drum

Dev fires up the drum twice a week: every Tuesday and Friday morning.

Walk down Chalk Lane around 9:00am if you want to catch the warm scent of toasted grain and caramel drifting out into the morning air. Grab a stool by the window; the noise is friendly.
